Cervical Cancer: the Pap Smear & HPV Story

How cytology screening and the discovery that HPV causes cervical cancer turned a leading cancer into a largely preventable one.

One of medicine’s great prevention success stories — and it runs on two tests this lab performs: the Pap smear and HPV testing.

What happened

In the 1940s, Dr George Papanicolaou introduced the “Pap smear” — a simple cytology test that spots abnormal cervical cells before they turn into cancer. Decades later, Prof. Harald zur Hausen showed that persistent infection with high-risk human papillomavirus (HPV), especially types 16 and 18, causes almost all cervical cancer — work recognised with the 2008 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Why it mattered

Wherever Pap screening was widely adopted, deaths from cervical cancer fell dramatically. Adding HPV testing — which detects the virus that drives the disease, often before any cell changes appear — makes screening even more sensitive. Together with HPV vaccination, the World Health Organization now treats cervical cancer as a cancer the world can work to eliminate.

Key points

  • Nearly all cervical cancers are caused by persistent high-risk HPV infection
  • The Pap (cytology) test detects pre-cancerous cell changes early, when they are treatable
  • HPV testing identifies the high-risk virus — sometimes before cell changes are visible
  • Liquid-based cytology (LBC) allows cytology and HPV testing from a single sample

How this connects to our testing

This lab performs both halves of this story — the Fluid Cytology / LBC Pap smear and HPV (molecular) testing. See the Test List, or contact the lab to discuss sample requirements. Pricing is shared on enquiry.
